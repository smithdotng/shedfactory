A good-looking website that nobody finds, or that loads slowly on a phone, isn't doing its job. Here's the checklist we work through on every site we build for clients.

## 1. It works brilliantly on a phone

Most visitors arrive on mobile, often on patchy data. Design for a small screen first: readable text, big tap targets, no sideways scrolling, and images sized for the device.

## 2. It's fast

Every extra second of loading costs visitors. Compress images, avoid heavy plugins and sliders you don't need, and host on infrastructure close to your customers.

## 3. Contact is one tap away

Make it easy to reach you the way your customers prefer. In Nigeria that very often means **WhatsApp**, alongside a phone number, email and a short form. Put contact options in the header, the footer and at the end of every key page.

## 4. It says clearly what you do

Within five seconds, a visitor should know what you offer, who it's for and what to do next. Lead with a clear headline and a single main call to action.

## 5. It looks good when shared

When someone pastes your link into WhatsApp, LinkedIn or X, the preview card is often their first impression. That card is controlled by **Open Graph tags** — a title, description and image set for each page. Without them, platforms guess, and the result is often a blank or broken preview.

## 6. Search engines can understand it

Good page titles and descriptions, a sitemap, sensible headings and real, helpful content all help people find you on Google. A blog — like this one — is one of the simplest ways to keep adding useful pages over time.

## 7. You can see what's working

Install analytics from day one so you know where visitors come from and which pages lead to enquiries.

## 8. Someone looks after it

Websites need updates, backups, security fixes and hosting that stays online. Plan for that from the start rather than after something breaks.
